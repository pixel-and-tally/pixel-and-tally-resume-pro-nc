It's becoming commonplace for employers to ask for virtual interviews. It's an excellent way to connect with a recruiter or hiring manager without having to travel, but virtual interviews are full of potential pitfalls. Employers who rely on them are looking for candidates who are tech savvy and forward-thinking. So if you're asked to do a Zoom, Teams, or FaceTime interview, make sure you're ready.

Here are our tips to help you prepare.

**1. Check your tech.** Get familiar with your webcam and microphone so you know how to use them. Do a test run with a family member or friend to make sure the video quality is on point and the audio is clear. Find the most flattering camera angle, one where you are looking directly into the camera. Eye contact is important, even if you aren't there in person.

**2. Prep your surroundings.** Guess what? The person on the other side of the screen is looking at more than just your face. Take that into consideration when setting up. They can also hear any noise in the room next to you. Set up in a quiet place where there will be no disruptions.

**3. Lighting is key.** Brighten up your interview by placing a diffused light or lamp behind the computer. This will light your face and keep the shadows at bay. Prospective employers will appreciate the extra effort!

**4. Dress to impress.** It may be tempting to wear jeans under that suit jacket. This one is a definite no-go. Even though you are not interviewing in person, appearance still matters. A lot. Dress professionally at every opportunity. This tells recruiters that you take the job and the interview seriously.

**5. Sit where you can speak freely.** Don't try to do a video interview from your current job. Show respect and common courtesy for your current employer by planning interviews offsite, so that you can speak openly about your work history and your expectations for a future position.
