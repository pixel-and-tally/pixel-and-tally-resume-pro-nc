Everyone is familiar with the quote, "The definition of insanity is doing the same thing over and over and expecting different results." This is particularly applicable to many job searches we see out there.

Below is a checklist for making sure your job search strategy covers all your bases and effectively delivers the results you are striving for.

## An example of an ineffective job search strategy

At a recent networking event, someone wanted insight into why he still hadn't found a job after 9 months of trying. He was asked to describe his weekly job search process. He responded that he goes to four job search websites, conducts searches based on his criteria, reviews the jobs that meet the criteria, and sends his resume and cover letter to each of the jobs he determines to be a good fit. Sometimes he receives a reply from an employer, but oftentimes he does not. Even still, he repeats this process each week.

If this sounds like your job search, ask yourself if what you are doing is working. Are you getting the results you want? If not, have you altered your approach? If not, stop for a minute, examine your process, and make the necessary changes. You need to determine why you are not rising to the top of the candidate list and fix your process so that you do!

## Job search checklist

**Strategy: you need to do the right things**

- What are the components of your job search?
- Do you have a plan with measurable targets (for example, the number of networking connections you will make and jobs you will apply for)?
- Is the plan written down?
- Are there other job search tactics you should add to your plan?

**Execution: you need to carry out your plan consistently each week**

- Which parts of your job search plan do you like to do?
- Which ones don't you like to do?
- Do you spend more time doing the things you enjoy (like searching for jobs online) and less time doing the things you dislike (like finding networking connections)?
- Do you document your actions each week?
- Are you meeting the targets in your plan?

**Connections: you need to connect with the people who receive your resume**

- Are you getting your resume and cover letter to the right person at the company?
- Are you spending time trying to find that person?
- Are you making connections at that company to ensure the hiring manager does review your resume?
- Is your profile on LinkedIn and other professional networking sites up to date?
- Are you using these sites to make connections with others?

If your job search is not yielding the results you want, when was the last time you reviewed your plan and made changes? Review the checklist above, decide what needs to change, and put those changes in place now.
