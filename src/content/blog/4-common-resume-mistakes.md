---
title: "4 Common Resume Mistakes"
date: 2017-03-06
categories: ["Resume Tips"]
---

Over years of writing resumes I have seen a number of mistakes. These are at the top of the list.

**Grammar and punctuation errors.** Too many people rely on spellcheck to catch spelling and punctuation errors. Although it's a great tool, spellcheck is not as capable as a thorough human eye. For example, "there," "their," and "they're" are all spelled correctly, but if you use the wrong one in a sentence, spellcheck will not catch it, and your resume is left with an error that makes a potential employer question your attention to detail. Also pay attention to apostrophes: make sure they are in the correct place (and that one is actually needed). Finally, spellcheckers often recommend changes that aren't needed. Running a spellcheck is a great start, but it is not totally reliable.

**Lack of resume keywords.** I cannot stress enough the importance of using keywords. If the keywords are not present, your resume will get tossed out of the process. Make sure your documented skills match the skill sets the employer requires so you do not lose out on job opportunities.

**Writing a job description.** Don't make the mistake of writing a job-description-style resume. Showcase your accomplishments instead, since that gives a potential employer a better understanding of what you can do for their organization. They want to know you have talents no other candidate can bring to the table.

**Overdesigning.** I know you might want to show off your design skills on your resume, but please keep things clean and simple. I have seen resumes with so many distracting designs that I had a hard time reading them. If the position calls for someone with creative skills, I'm sure you'll get the chance to show your flair, but the resume is not that stage. As Walt Whitman said, "Simplicity is the glory of expression."
