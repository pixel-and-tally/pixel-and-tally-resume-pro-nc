Please don't tell me you are going to wing it for your big interview!

You may not know what questions will be asked, but you *can* be prepared for anything the hiring manager throws at you. And believe it or not, it takes a lot more than Googling a list of common interview topics. You have to make a great first impression appearance-wise (tip: use an iron!), know your target company, understand the products or services they provide, and know exactly how you plan to tell them you're the perfect fit for the job.

Here are some of our favorite tips to help you bring your A-game to the interview.

## Know your audience

- **Do your research.** Learn as much as you can about the company you're interviewing with. That means searching different sources like the company website, magazines, online articles, press releases, and social media accounts, and talking to people who have worked for them or done business with them. This will give you a better understanding of the big picture, both good and bad.
- **Get a sense of who the company is.** Each company has its own personality and culture. You can understand a lot about how they treat each other and outsiders by reading their blogs (if they have them) and combing through their social media. Learn their voice. If it's in line with yours, or what you hope to become, speak to that in your interview. It shows you'll fit in with the company culture, and that means a lot, especially when you're working long hours in high-pressure situations.
- **Check their social accounts.** Social media can be an excellent resource for interview content. Are employees posting about an upcoming event? Are they celebrating something great that happened with an account? These can be excellent conversation starters and show the hiring manager that you are invested in getting to know your potential coworkers and the company as a whole.
- **If the company sells a product or service, try it out.** Nothing will teach you more about a company than being a customer first. It's easy to get on board with something you love, and it gives you a unique perspective as you begin the interview process. If you have a terrible experience, think long and hard about working there. Whether it's the product or service itself or some other part of the purchase process, you have two options: walk away, or use the opportunity to explain how you could improve the department you are interviewing for. As a customer first, you have insight other candidates may not, and that could play to your advantage.
- **Ask questions when the interview is scheduled.** When you get the call or email to schedule an interview, don't get so excited that you forget to ask questions. This one step will help you prepare more than any other. First, ask who you will be interviewing with. That lets you do some reconnaissance: who are they in the company, what role do they play, and what is their personality or interview style? Most of this you can find with a little digging. Second, ask what type of interview it will be. Will you be in an office or meet off-site, like at a coffee shop? Will it be a virtual interview? Is it a classic Q&A interview, or will there be brain teasers? It's totally fair to ask, and most recruiters and hiring managers will be upfront about it.

**The takeaway:** research and preparation will help you stand out from the crowd. Go the extra mile. I promise very few others will be running that race with you!
