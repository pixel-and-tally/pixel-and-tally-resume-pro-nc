---
title: "Stop Cramming Everything Into Your Resume"
date: 2017-08-15
categories: ["Resume Tips"]
---

A common mistake job seekers make when preparing their resumes is trying to cram in every detail about their experience, skills, data, and interests. By all means, you want your resume to be as professional as it can be as you offer a substantive presentation of what you can bring to an organization.

A *substantive* presentation, not an *exhaustive* one!

Your resume should be a curated document of your experience, skills, and interests, with your selections informed by the most impressive and relevant parts of your background and the requirements of the position you are applying for.

It should not list every mundane job responsibility, every course you took in college, or all of your hobbies and interests. In many cases, it is not even necessary to list every position you have held. If a piece of information on your resume does not make you a stronger, more interesting candidate, either reframe it or remove it.

Removing extra information frees up valuable real estate on your resume, letting you list and elaborate on things that *do* strengthen you as a candidate (not to mention shortening your document, making it more readable and less likely to be tossed aside for being too long). Go into a bit of detail about the implementation project you led and what the results were. Note the relevant professional associations you belong to and your certifications. Using resume real estate that way is invariably better than focusing on your junior position from 20 years ago or stating that you made copies and did routine data entry.

You have much to offer. The key is communicating clearly what you would bring to an organization, with a substantive presentation, not an exhaustive one.
